Sexual health

What is good sexual health (WHO)?

  • A state of physical, emotional, mental and social well-being in relation to sexuality — not merely the absence of disease, dysfunction or infirmity.
  • Requires a positive and respectful approach to sexuality and sexual relationships, and the possibility of pleasurable and safe sexual experiences free of coercion, discrimination and violence.

Describe the benefits of good sexual health.

  • Improves physical health outcomes
  • Improves mental health outcomes
  • Positively influences social and emotional wellbeing

Describe the consequences of unprotected sex.

  • Unplanned pregnancies and abortions
  • Psychological consequences including from sexual coercion and abuse
  • Poor educational, social and economic opportunities for teenage mothers, young fathers and their children
  • HIV transmission
  • Cervical and other genital cancers
  • Hepatitis, chronic liver disease and liver cancer
  • Recurrent genital herpes/warts
  • Pelvic inflammatory disease → ectopic pregnancies and infertility
  • Adverse maternal and neonatal pregnancy outcomes

Describe three ways we can prevent poor sexual health.

  • Contraception
  • HPV immunisation
  • HIV prevention
  • Education / Health Literacy

Describe the clinician’s role in prevention of poor sexual health.

  • Provide information about contraception options
  • Educate patients about risks and consequences of unprotected sex
  • Facilitate prompt access to contraception and sexual health services

How can we protect against STIs?

  • Barrier protection prevents against all STIs.
  • NOTE: Only 1/3 of young people (15–29) use barrier protection consistently; only 71% thought it was the best form of protection against STIs.

Describe the population health approach to prevention of poor sexual health.

  • 2019: relationships education compulsory for all primary school pupils.
  • Relationships and sex education made compulsory for secondary school pupils in England.

Describe how unplanned pregnancies can be prevented.

Preventing unintended pregnancies using contraception is essential for improving multiple health outcomes.

Describe three factors associated with unplanned pregnancies.

  • Smoking
  • Drug use
  • Depression
  • NOTE: Unplanned pregnancies are more common in young, single women

Describe the characteristics of teenage pregnancies (before age 18).

  • Majority are unplanned
  • 50% result in abortion
  • Huge physical and psychological impact

Describe the consequences of teenage pregnancies (before age 18).

  • Higher risk of poor antenatal health
  • Lower birth weight babies
  • Higher infant mortality rate
  • Lower likelihood of finishing education
  • Less likely to find employment
  • More likely to live in poverty
  • 3× more likely to suffer from post-natal depression

Describe the predisposing factors to teenage pregnancies.

  • In or leaving care
  • Homelessness
  • Involvement in crime
  • Low educational attainment
  • Social deprivation
  • NOTE: These individuals are more vulnerable to coercive and exploitative sexual relationships

Describe the approach doctors should take in sexual health consultations.

Non-judgmental, opportunistic, holistic and empathetic — informing patients and facilitating decisions/access to support and services.

Describe the role of the HPV vaccine.

Girls and boys aged 12–13 offered HPV vaccine as part of NHS immunisation. Helps protect against cervical cancer; some mouth/throat (head and neck) cancers; some anal and genital cancers; also genital warts.

Describe the role of PrEP.

Pre-exposure prophylaxis: HIV drugs taken by an HIV-negative person to lower the chance of becoming infected with HIV in the future.

Describe the role of PEP.

Post-exposure prophylaxis: anti-HIV medication prescribed to an HIV-negative person after potential exposure to protect them from infection.

Describe a problem with sexual health treatments.

Stigma associated with sexual health and STIs.

Describe the demographic most affected by STIs.

Heterosexual people between 15 and 24 years old.

Describe how MECC can be used to improve poor sexual health.

  • Initiate conversations about sexual health
  • Increase screening uptake
  • Increase subsequent diagnosis and treatment of STIs

Describe how lifestyle factors can impact sexual health.

  • Smoking, prolonged alcohol use, stress and poor mental health can lead to sexual dysfunction.
  • Drug and alcohol use are associated with risky sexual behaviour (on an individual basis).

Women's health and inequalities

What is the difference between sex and gender?

  • Sex (UK Government): biological — anatomy, chromosomes, hormones; generally male or female; assigned at birth.
  • Gender: social construct relating to behaviours; gender identity is personal/internal; man/woman commonly applied to gender not sex; may not match sex assigned at birth.

Life expectancy females vs males.

  • In the UK, life expectancy is higher for females than males.
  • Life expectancy is stalling and even declining for women in most areas, particularly those in more deprived areas.
  • Healthy life expectancy has also been declining for women since 2010.

Describe how certain conditions can affect females more than males.

  • Obesity increases cancer risk — fat tissue is inflammatory, produces sex hormones (oestrogen), increases growth factors (insulin) → promote rapid cell division.
  • Breast, uterine and endometrial cancer most closely linked to obesity as a risk factor and mainly affect women.

Which gender is more at risk of obesity?

Women.

Which gender is more at risk of dementia and why?

Women — potentially due to the protective role of oestrogen on brain cells which decreases following menopause.

Which sex is less physically active?

Females.

Describe common barriers to females being less physically active.

  • Fear of judgment
  • Lacking confidence
  • Lacking time

Describe how outcomes of myocardial infarction differ between men and women.

Women are at higher risk of initial misdiagnosis following myocardial infarction.

Describe what women’s inclusion in clinical trials is like.

Women have not always been represented equally in research (e.g. thalidomide — birth defects not discovered in time due to trials predominantly on men).

Describe problems women face in the working world and consequences.

  • Gender pay gap
  • Women overrepresented in lower paid, lower skilled jobs; higher rates of poverty in older age; more likely to suffer financial insecurity
  • Reasons: ‘motherhood penalty’; jobs done by women undervalued; men hold more of the most senior roles

Describe the problem of violence against women and girls.

  • Young women and girls more likely than boys/men to experience sexual abuse, physical abuse or neglect.
  • Around a quarter of women experience domestic abuse or violence at some point in their lives.

What is the Women’s Health Strategy?

A plan outlining a 10-year strategy for women’s health in England.

Describe the six-point plan in the Women’s Health Strategy.

  • Ensuring women’s voices are heard — representation at all levels; listened to by HCPs
  • Improving access to services — e.g. endometriosis, autism in women
  • Addressing disparities in outcomes among women — ethnicity and socio-economic status
  • Better information and education on women’s health — for patients and professionals
  • Greater understanding of how women’s health affects workplace experience — normalising conversations on periods and menopause
  • Supporting more research — improve representation of women of all demographics; address research gaps

Relationships and health

Where is oxytocin produced and what is it released in response to?

  • Produced in the hypothalamus; released from the posterior pituitary in response to social connection, sexual intercourse, labour and childbirth.

Describe the effects of oxytocin release.

  • Social bonding
  • Prosocial behaviour
  • Empathy
  • Elevated mood
  • Stress reduction
  • All important for survival evolutionarily

Describe the importance of good quality relationships.

  • Important aspect of human life; social networks provide resilience and promote recovery from illness.
  • Vital source of social capital — bonds providing emotional, cultural and socioeconomic support.

Describe the difference between women’s and men’s friendships.

  • Women more likely to have broader and more intimate friendships than men.
  • More likely to discuss personal matters and feel more supported during times of stress.

Describe the effect of good quality social connections.

  • Live longer and have healthier lifestyle behaviours
  • Suffer less from mental health issues; better recoveries from stressors
  • Better cardiovascular health and stronger immune system
  • Lower severity of perceived physical pain

Describe the effect of unhealthy relationships.

  • Equally adverse effect on health
  • Negative aspects more likely to affect women and people with lower SES
  • Women report higher psychological distress and negative interactions despite broader/more intimate relationships

What are the three types of social connection according to the UCLA Loneliness Scale?

  • Intimate: people who personally love and support you
  • Relational: people you see regularly and may share an interest with
  • Collective: people who share a common affiliation or group membership

Describe which gender is more at risk of domestic abuse.

Women.

Describe the effects of domestic abuse during pregnancy.

Increases risk of: miscarriage; infection; premature birth; injury or death of the baby following birth.

Describe how people who experience domestic abuse can be supported.

  • Elicit information in a sensitive and empathetic manner
  • Signpost to appropriate sources for guidance and safety
  • Plan follow-up with flexible appointments; refer to domestic abuse support worker as needed
  • Address fear of social/child protection involvement; reassure confidentiality where appropriate
  • Allow autonomy over decisions without pressure
  • Respect decisions and remain non-judgemental

What is non-volitional sex?

  • Any sexual behaviour that violates a person’s right to choose freely when and with whom to have sex and what sexual behaviours to engage in.
  • Includes rape, forced sex, childhood sexual abuse, sexual violence, sex trafficking.

Support resources (domestic violence / non-volitional sex / no fixed abode).

  • Women: Freephone National Domestic Abuse Helpline (Refuge)
  • Men: Men’s advice line or ManKind
  • LGBTQ+: Galop
  • NHS webpage on Domestic Violence
  • Imperial Sexual Violence Support Team; SARCs / The Havens in London
  • CLASH team sexual health outreach (homeless, sex workers, inclusion health)
  • Doctors of the World GP Access Cards — everyone entitled to free NHS GP; no address/ID not grounds to refuse registration

Maternal health

Describe the importance of the pregnancy period.

  • Women often particularly reflective on health behaviours and more amenable to healthy lifestyle change.
  • Changes can be long-lasting and impact positively on future health for mother and baby.

Describe a factor that plays a major role in foetal development.

Maternal nutrition plays a crucial role in maintaining health of the developing foetus.

Recall two supplements recommended during pregnancy.

  • Folic acid
  • Vitamin D
  • Naturally occurring micronutrients supplemented during pregnancy

Describe the impact of folic acid supplements.

Recommended prior to conception and up to 12 weeks of pregnancy to reduce risk of neural tube defects such as spina bifida.

Describe the impact of vitamin D supplements.

Recommended throughout pregnancy and continued after if breastfeeding — promote bone health in the foetus and reduce risk of rickets.

Recall three folate-rich foods.

  • Green leafy vegetables
  • Peas
  • Kidney beans
  • Chickpeas

Describe three impacts of physical activity during pregnancy.

  • Decreased risk of hypertension in pregnancy, pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes
  • Reduced severity of depressive symptoms during and after pregnancy — NOTE: 1 in 5 women experience a mental health problem during pregnancy or first year after birth
  • No evidence of adverse effects for healthy pregnant women undertaking moderate-intensity physical activity

Describe three impacts of drinking alcohol during pregnancy.

  • Early miscarriage
  • Premature birth
  • Restricted growth
  • Stillbirth
  • Cognitive delay
  • Foetal alcohol spectrum disorders
  • Foetal alcohol syndrome
  • NOTE: FASD are irreversible and avoidable

Describe three characteristics of foetal alcohol syndrome.

  • Small stature
  • Facial features (microcephaly, microphthalmia, short palpebral fissures, epicanthal folds, small/flat midface, flat elongated philtrum, thin upper lip, small chin)
  • Abnormal palmar creases
  • Cardiac defects
  • Joint contractures/stiffness
  • Cognitive deficits

Describe the prevalence and health burden of smoking during pregnancy.

Causes up to 2200 premature births, 5000 miscarriages and 3000 perinatal deaths every year in the UK.

Describe three impacts of smoking during pregnancy.

  • Increased risk of low birth weight and premature birth
  • Increased risk of child developing respiratory conditions, diabetes, obesity, ENT problems

What is the microbiome? (maternal section recap)

  • Genetic material of all microbes that live on or within the human body.
  • Interacts with nervous and immune systems; important role in health and disease.
  • Factors affecting diversity/health: genes, age, environment, stress, diet, physical activity, infant feeding method, birthing process.
