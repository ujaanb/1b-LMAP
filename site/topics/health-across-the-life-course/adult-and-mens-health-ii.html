3.6 Adult and Men's Health II

Gambling as a public health issue: scale, regulation benefits, and biological/psychological/social/demographic/environmental risk factors.

Sections
  1. Gambling

Gambling

What is gambling?

The wagering of money or something of value on a game of chance, with the intent of winning money or material goods.

Describe the magnitude of gambling as a problem in the UK.

  • 48% of the British population surveyed had participated in gambling in the past month.
  • Average British gambler spends £11/week or £49/month.
  • 27% of 11- to 17-year-olds had spent their own money on any gambling activity in the prior 12 months.
  • The National Lottery is the most popular form of gambling in the UK.

What is problem gambling?

Gambling to a degree that compromises, disrupts, or damages family, personal, or recreational pursuits.

Describe how socioeconomic benefits can be derived from a well-regulated gambling industry.

  • State-run lotteries fund social/community programmes — National Lottery Community Fund & Heritage Fund provided over £1.7 billion to good causes (Apr 2022–Mar 2023).
  • Gambling venues: major source of employment and tax revenues (e.g. Macau, Las Vegas).
  • Venues can provide important social activity (e.g. bingo halls where alternatives are limited).

Describe biological risk factors for developing a gambling habit.

  • Genetic predisposition — impulsivity/addiction tendency
  • Neurochemical imbalances — gambling stimulates dopamine release, creating dependency cycle

Describe psychological risk factors.

  • Cognitive biases — gambler’s fallacy; chasing losses
  • Personality traits — high impulsivity, sensation-seeking, low self-control
  • Mental health conditions — anxiety, depression, substance misuse, delusional disorders; gambling as coping
  • Design of games — fast-paced immediate rewards (slots); near misses; intermittent rewards; small winnings less than wagered amount

Describe social risk factors.

  • Peer, familial, cultural influences — normalised when peers/family gamble; socially acceptable in some cultures
  • Escapism/entertainment — loneliness, major life events, lack of hobbies

Describe demographic risk factors.

  • Age and sex — younger individuals, especially males, more likely to engage in risky gambling
  • Lower education/awareness about gambling risks
  • Economic difficulties/unemployment — gambling perceived as way to improve situation

Describe environmental risk factors.

  • Advertising and marketing — aggressive promotions
  • Access and availability/technology — online gambling and mobile apps provide anonymity and accessibility; easy access increases engagement
