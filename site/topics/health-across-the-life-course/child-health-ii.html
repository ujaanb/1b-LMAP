Live session recap

Population thresholds for BMI classification are lower than individual thresholds — why?

To pick up obesity/overweight children much earlier.

Key live themes.

  • Wider determinants of health
  • Making Every Contact Count (MECC)
  • LEAP goals — Local, Enjoyable and Practical

NCMP and child BMI

What is the National Child Measurement Programme?

  • Opt-out programme measuring height and weight of over one million primary school children in England in Reception (age 4–5) and Year 6 (age 10–11).
  • Established in 2005/6.

Describe how Child BMI data is used.

  • For population health monitoring, BMI is compared to British 1990 (UK90) growth reference data.
  • Population monitoring cut-offs are lower than individual clinical cut-offs.

Describe two factors that affect the prevalence of obesity across the population.

Obesity prevalence varies by ethnic group and by level of deprivation.

Lifestyle consultations and weight support tiers

Describe three communication strategies during lifestyle/weight consultations with children and families.

  • A strengths-based approach
  • A whole family approach
  • Goals should be short-term and specific — remember LEAP
  • Signposting and follow-up

What are Tier 1 Healthy Weight Support Services?

Umbrella term for a variety of programmes promoting healthy behaviours that provide universal support for the general population.

Describe the characteristics of Tier 1 services.

  • Universal support for general population
  • Community and school based
  • Includes national and local initiatives
  • Examples: walking/cycling programmes, cooking, online education, school games, health promotion workshops, marketing/campaigns, behaviour-change apps (Sugar Smart, Couch to 5K), Healthy Schools London, Healthy Early Years Award, Unicef Baby Friendly, The Daily Mile

Describe two strengths of Tier 1 services.

  • Promotes healthy behaviours
  • Supports primary prevention
  • Large variety of services
  • Accessible and available (ideally)

Describe two limitations of Tier 1 services.

  • Lack of personalisation (may not address individual needs / culturally competent)
  • May exclude those with additional needs
  • Resources can be spread thin

What are Tier 2 Healthy Weight Support Services?

Community-based, structured programmes designed to help individuals with overweight or mild obesity adopt healthier habits, particularly diet, physical activity and lifestyle modifications.

Describe the characteristics of Tier 2 services.

  • Minimum eligibility ≥ 91st centile
  • Mostly commissioned by local authorities; delivered in community, leisure or school settings
  • Mostly multicomponent lifestyle approaches (diet, activity, behaviour change)
  • Delivered over 8–12 weeks in predominantly group settings; family focused for younger ages
  • Referral: self, health professional or school
  • Followed up for 12 months or more

Describe two strengths of Tier 2 services.

  • More tailored and structured support
  • Family focused healthy behaviour change with some follow-up
  • Upskills families for self-management

Describe two limitations of Tier 2 services.

  • Resource intensive — more costly and requires more time
  • Referral pathways can be unclear; accessibility difficulties
  • Although some follow-up, relatively short-term

Tackling obesity: systems, life course, legislation

Describe three ways we can make a Whole Systems Approach to tackling obesity.

  • Reducing congestion and car use — better air quality
  • Healthy school meals and good quality PE
  • Health in all Policies
  • Connected public transport and active travel
  • Affordable, accessible and inclusive leisure services
  • Healthy affordable and accessible food
  • Safe and welcoming green spaces

Describe three ways we can take a life course approach to tackling obesity.

  • Healthy weight in pregnancy
  • Universal checks
  • Breastfeeding & First Foods
  • MECC
  • Raising the issue of weight
  • School Food & Healthy Schools London
  • NHS Health Checks
  • Weight Management Support
  • Healthy ageing

Describe three ways obesity is being tackled using legislation.

  • Soft Drinks Industry Levy
  • Introduction of calorie labelling
  • Legislation to restrict placement of foods high in fat, sugar or salt in supermarkets to reduce impulse purchases
  • Banning advertisement of less healthy products on TV/on-demand before the watershed (9pm–5.30am) and online 24/7 from October 2025
  • Transport for London (TfL) and Local Healthier Advertising Policy
