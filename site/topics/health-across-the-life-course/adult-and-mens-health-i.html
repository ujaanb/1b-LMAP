3.5 Adult and Men's Health I

Stress, men's health inequalities, self-care frameworks, stepped care for mental health, and substance/alcohol misuse.

Stress

Describe the effects that stress can have on individuals.

  • Not all stress is negative — a degree of stress can help improve performance (e.g. deadlines).
  • Beyond a certain level, performance declines; ongoing chronic stress can be toxic.

Inequalities in men's health in the UK

Describe the general health inequalities men face compared to women.

  • Males live shorter lives than females on average (higher mortality).
  • Males suffer life-limiting disease sooner.
  • The gap between male and female life expectancy is actually greater in higher-income countries.

Describe the driver behind health inequalities between men and women in the UK.

  • Social constructs of gender and identity mean men are subject to different risks and demonstrate different health-seeking behaviours.
  • NOTE: Biology is not as significant a driver as one might initially think.

What is the difference between sex and gender?

  • Sex: biological — anatomy, chromosomes, hormones; male/female; assigned at birth.
  • Gender: social construct relating to behaviours; gender identity personal/internal; man/woman applied to gender.

Describe the difference in mortality rate between males and females.

Mortality is consistently higher for males, although the gap is closing a little.

Describe the difference in health-seeking behaviour between males and females.

  • Men are far less likely to seek professional help when they need it.
  • This reluctance is an important factor driving inequalities of health outcome between genders.

Describe the difference in suicide attempts and mortality rates.

  • Females are more likely to attempt suicide but males are more likely to complete a suicide attempt.

Describe cultural changes that can improve suicide rates for males.

  • Workplace initiatives
  • Social prescribing — men tend to connect ‘shoulder-to-shoulder’ rather than face-to-face (sharing interests / working together)

Self-care

What is self-care?

What people do for themselves to establish and maintain health and to prevent and deal with illness. Many definitions exist.

Recall the two frameworks used to conceptualise self-care.

  • Seven pillars of self-care
  • Self-care continuum

Describe what is meant by each of the seven pillars of self-care.

  • Knowledge and health literacy: capacity to obtain, process and understand basic health information
  • Mental wellbeing: managing changes in mental health and self-awareness (BMI, BP, screening)
  • Physical activity: walking to shops, stairs, active travel, exercise/sport
  • Healthy eating: balanced, high-fibre diet rich in nutrients with appropriate calorie intake
  • Risk avoidance: quitting tobacco/alcohol, vaccination
  • Good hygiene: oral/dental hygiene, hand washing, good sleeping practices
  • Rational use of products and services: taking medicines properly; responsible OTC use

What is the self-care continuum?

Shows that we are each placed along a continuum describing the extent that we are reliant on external support and resources.

Modifiable causes of chronic diseases.

Improved self-care can prevent up to 93% of T2DM cases, 81% of heart attacks, 50% of stroke cases and 36% of all new cancers.

Describe how self-care can tackle communicable diseases (COVID example).

  • Risk avoidance — school closures, lockdown, social distancing
  • Good hygiene — hand washing, sanitiser, contact tracing app
  • Awareness and literacy — health literacy, public health messaging, shielding high-risk groups

Describe three ways self-care is in our society today.

  • Sustainable Development Goal 3
  • WHO Guidelines
  • Country level (NHS Guidelines)

What is the Health in All Policies (HiAP) approach?

Championed by the WHO — whenever a policy is being drafted, it should also consider how it can support the healthy well-being of individuals and society.

Describe some self-care enablers.

  • Health apps
  • Wearables — BP monitor, sleep apnoea monitor, glucometer, breath analysis, heart rate, pedometer

Describe why self-care from cradle to grave is important.

  • Key role in promoting vitality in ageing
  • Directly relevant to lifestyle medicine & workplace health
  • Self-care behaviours are a huge area of focus today

What is the stepped care model?

Framework to organise provision of services and support patients, carers and practitioners in identifying and accessing the most effective interventions.

Describe the general principle of the stepped care model of mental health.

  • Least intrusive, most effective intervention provided first; if no benefit or declined, offer appropriate intervention from the next step.
  • As severity worsens/increases, intensity of intervention increases.

Describe what Step 1 and Step 2 encompass.

  • Step 1: care before accessing health/social services — friends/family, community, self-help, spiritual support, self-help groups, occupational advice, MH organisations/charities, helplines, apps, housing, employment, leisure, carer support.
  • Step 2: low-intensity psychosocial interventions, psychological interventions, medication and referral for further assessment (e.g. computerised CBT; social prescribing; caseworker support).

Substance misuse

Describe risk factors that increase risk of substance misuse.

  • Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs)
  • Poor mental health

Describe problems caused by drugs that are smoked.

Lung problems (e.g. COPD).

Describe problems caused by drugs that are injected.

Blood clots, bacterial infections of the skin, viral infections.

What are club drugs?

  • Group of drugs most often used by teens and young adults at bars, nightclubs, concerts and parties to heighten sensory perceptions and reduce inhibitions.
  • Include MDMA (ecstasy), herbal ecstasy, Rohypnol, GHB, ketamine.

What drug class do Co-codamol and Tramadol fall under?

Opiates — same family as heroin and morphine.

Which drug is methadone prescribed as a safer substitute for?

Heroin.

What is alcohol misuse?

  • Drinking more than 14 units per week or binge drinking.
  • Binge drinking: more than eight units on a single occasion for men or six units for women.

Who is at greater risk of alcohol misuse?

Men and the middle-aged (ages 55–64).

Describe the relationship between SES and alcohol misuse.

  • Inverse relationship between SES and alcohol misuse — proportion drinking over recommended limits increases as affluence increases.
  • However, deprived communities experience greater harm — the alcohol harm paradox (multiplier effect: alcohol interacts with other factors causing ill health in low SES groups).
