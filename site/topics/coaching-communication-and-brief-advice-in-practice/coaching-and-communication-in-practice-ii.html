Recap of COM-B

What is the COM-B model?

Particularly relevant when considering behaviour change interventions and the sustainability of new behaviours. Behaviour is a result of:

  • Capability — physical (including skills) and psychological (including knowledge required to take steps towards behaviour change)
  • Opportunity — physical (e.g. time and cost) and social (e.g. supportive companion or group)
  • Motivation — reflective (considering long-term benefits) and automatic (using this reflection to see the behaviour change as a ‘want’ not just a ‘need’)

Describe how the COM-B model can be applied in health coaching scenarios.

During health coaching conversations, we can help people further explore the influences of capability, opportunity and motivation, with the aim of optimising these in the direction of behaviour change.

COM-B coaching questions (from lecture diagram).

  • Capability: What part of this do you have control over?
  • Opportunity: What are your options? Which of these options seems the most feasible?
  • Motivation: Imagine you are at your most resourceful — what do you say to yourself about your issue? What’s stopped you achieving your ideal outcome? Imagine you have achieved your goal — what does that look like for you?

Experimenting with a health coaching approach

Describe the problem with this set of questions in a health coaching setting.

Problem-focused set:

  • What is the problem?
  • Why has this problem happened?
  • Why haven’t you addressed the problem yet?
  • Who else is this affecting?
  • What will happen if you don’t address this problem?

Describe how this set of questions is better for a health coaching setting.

  • What’s the issue?
  • What do you want ideally around this issue?
  • Plus COM-B-aligned questions above (control, options/feasibility, resourceful self-talk, barriers, ideal outcome visualisation).

These are more solution-/resource-oriented and map onto capability, opportunity and motivation.
