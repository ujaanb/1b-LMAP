Recap of key terminology

What is health coaching?

  • The use of evidence-based skilful conversation, clinical strategies, and interventions to actively engage patients in health behaviour change to better self-manage their health.
  • As an umbrella term, it includes a wide range of applications — standalone intervention, integrated into clinical practice, or part of a system of care.

What is motivational interviewing?

  • A collaborative, goal-oriented style of communication with attention to the language of change.
  • Designed to strengthen internal motivation for and commitment to a specific goal related to a specific behaviour by eliciting and exploring the person’s own reasons for change within an atmosphere of acceptance and compassion.

What is a brief intervention?

  • An evidence-based, structured conversation to motivate and support an individual to think about and/or plan a change in a health behaviour.
  • It may also involve a referral for further interventions, directing people to other options, or more intensive support.

What is very brief advice (VBA)?

  • Takes approx 30 seconds to a couple of minutes.
  • Enables delivery of information to people, or signposting them to sources of further help.
  • May also include raising awareness of risks, or providing encouragement and support for change.
  • Used as a smoking cessation strategy.

What is personalised care?

  • A model of health and care centred around people having choice and control over the way their care is planned and delivered.
  • Based on ‘what matters’ to patients and their individual strengths and needs.
  • Social Prescribing, Health & Wellbeing Coaches and Care Coordinators within primary care networks (PCNs) are core components of the Personalised Care model.

Inclusion health and the Lived Experience Project

What is inclusion health?

  • A term used to describe any population group that is socially excluded.
  • Can include those who experience homelessness, people with substance and alcohol dependence, vulnerable migrants, people who sell sex, victims of modern slavery, people in contact with the criminal justice system, and people from Gypsy, Roma and Traveller communities — but can also include other socially excluded groups.

Describe the impacts of being in an inclusion health group.

  • Experience a significantly more severe burden of morbidity and mortality than the general public.
  • Individuals often have multiple and complex needs with various clinical and social challenges.
  • NOTE: As healthcare practitioners, you will work with patients from socially excluded groups throughout your career. Hence it is important to have awareness of the health needs, beliefs and outcomes of these vulnerable groups.
