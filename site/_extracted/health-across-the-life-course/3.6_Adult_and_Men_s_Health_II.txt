•
Cognitive biases
◦
Beliefs like the “gambler’s fallacy” lead individuals to think

chances of future wins. Gambling to recover previous losses

spiral of behaviour
•
Personality traits
◦
Traits like high impulsivity and sensation-seeking are 
strongly linked to gambling
problems. Low self-control, difficulty managing urges or 
delaying gratification
exacerbates gambling behaviours

Anxiety, depression, substance misuse, delusional disorders

Describe the social risk factors that increase one’s risk of developing a 
gambling habit.
•
Peer, familial, cultural influences
◦
Gambling behaviours are normalised when peers or family 
members frequently gamble. Gambling is socially 
acceptable or desirable in some cultures, increasing 
participation.
•
Gambling as escapism or entertainment
◦
Loneliness and lack of social support leads individuals to 
gamble for connection or escape. Major life events (e.g., job 
loss, divorce) trigger gambling as a coping
mechanism. Lack of engaging activities or hobbies pushes 
individuals toward
gambling as entertainment.
Describe the demographic risk factors that increase one’s risk of 
developing a gambling habit.
•
Age and sex 
◦
Younger individuals, especially males, are more likely to 
engage in risky gambling
behaviours
•
Education and Awareness
◦
Lower levels of education and awareness about gambling 
risks contribute to higher vulnerability
•
Economic difficulties
◦
Financial difficulties or unemployment lead individuals to 
perceive gambling as a way to improve their situation
